# ATHENA — Active Grants and Funding Register

**Date:** 30 September 2026
**For:** ATHENA, a women-focused platform built in Brisbane, Queensland; pre-launch, pre-revenue, founder-led; entity structure still to be decided.
**Method:** Ten research agents each swept one funding source (federal business and innovation; federal social policy; Queensland innovation and small business; Queensland women, DFV, mental health and community; South East Queensland councils; philanthropic foundations; corporate foundations, credits and prizes; accelerators, angels and VC; social enterprise and impact finance; sector-specific funders and awards). They produced 201 candidates, 194 after de-duplication. Every candidate was then handed to a verifier that opened the official page and confirmed, as of 30 September 2026, whether the program exists, whether it is open, its closing date, its amount, and whether a Queensland, pre-revenue, founder-led company can apply — correcting the finder where the page disagreed. A final critic searched for what the sweep had missed and added 18 more, which were verified the same way. 54 agents, 2,964 searches and page fetches, 58 minutes. 204 distinct programs remain after merging duplicates; all of them are in Appendix A with the page each was checked against.

**Verdicts across the 204:** 23 open now with a closing date · 86 rolling or always open · 17 opening on a known date · 58 recurring programs between rounds · 14 that could not be verified from an official page · 15 closed, discontinued or not open to a Queensland company.

**Fit for ATHENA as it is today:** 23 strong · 71 possible with a condition · 119 need a structural change (a charity, a certified social enterprise, women-led ownership, trading revenue, or a university partner).

> **Read the limits before acting.** The agents' shared web-search budget ran out partway through the run, so the later checks were direct fetches of official pages. Many Queensland Government domains (advance.qld.gov.au, women.qld.gov.au, families.qld.gov.au, dtet.qld.gov.au, the Grants Finder), the ATO, GrantConnect and the Community Grants Hub sit behind bot protection and refused the fetcher. Where that happened the entry says so and its status comes from a ministerial statement, the 2026-27 Budget papers, business.gov.au, a SmartyGrants page or a named third-party listing. **Confirm every closing date on the official page before you spend a day on an application.** Section 6 lists what could not be verified at all.

## Bottom line

**There is no open federal or Queensland Government cash grant today that a pre-revenue, for-profit tech startup can apply to on its own.** Ignite Ideas is between rounds with no Round 14 announced. The Industry Growth Program is paused to new applications. Business Basics has closed with no next round; Business Boost and the Business Growth Fund need $300,000 and $500,000 of prior-year turnover respectively. Boosting Female Founders, the eSafety tech-abuse grants and Accelerating Female Founders have ended. The whole federal social-policy lane — DSS, the Office for Women, eSafety, Health — was walked in full on GrantConnect (128 current and 152 forecast opportunities) and contains nothing a Pty Ltd can apply for; every relevant round needs an ACNC-registered not-for-profit or a university, or is by invitation.

**What ATHENA can apply for this week, as a company:** the Startmate accelerator (A$120,000, closes 1 November), the refundable R&D Tax Incentive (about 43.5 cents back on every eligible R&D dollar, from the day a company exists), the Antler Brisbane residency (February 2027), eight venture funds and two angel networks that accept cold pitches, two equity-crowdfunding platforms, and roughly twenty in-kind programs (cloud, AI, analytics, support-desk and design credits worth well over US$50,000 in the first year, plus the 15% app-store fee tiers). Plus free government advisory that is genuinely useful before launch: a cyber health check, AI-adoption advice, and mentoring for investment and pitch.

**The register is gated by four facts about ATHENA, and each is ATHENA's to change:**

1. **It is not yet a company.** The R&D Tax Incentive, ESIC investor concessions, CSIRO Kick-Start, every VC and angel door, equity crowdfunding, the Queensland Mental Health Commission's sponsorship, Ignite Ideas and Queensland Treasury's impact programs all require a Pty Ltd (most also an ABN and GST). Two clocks start at incorporation: R&D spend only counts once a company incurs it, and the Queensland co-investment fund's six-month ownership test runs from when the cap table is set. **Incorporate first; nothing else in this document moves faster.**
2. **It is not women-led in ownership or executive role.** About half of the women-focused private and state programs are gated on the founder or majority owner being a woman, not on serving women: the Queensland Female Founders Co-Investment Fund (A$50,000–200,000, open to 30 June 2027, needs 51% female ownership and a woman in a primary executive role for six continuous months), Scale Investors (A$100,000–500,000 pre-seed, needs a woman co-founder with equal equity and a C-suite title), Coralus, the Rural Women's Award, Women in Digital, AusMumpreneur, Cartier. A woman co-founder with equal equity and an executive title opens most of them, and the earlier the six-month clock starts the better.
3. **It has no charitable vehicle.** Almost every philanthropic, corporate-foundation, council community and Queensland social-policy grant — People First Bank Foundation ($80,000, DFV named, closes 23 October), Safe Futures Delivery Grants (up to $600,000, January–February 2027), Empowering Queensland Women, the Gambling Community Benefit Fund, Perpetual IMPACT, Bupa, Equity Trustees, the Queensland Community Foundation, every SEQ council community grant — requires an ACNC-registered not-for-profit, usually with DGR Item 1. Registering one takes months, so it will not reach the October–December 2026 rounds. The faster route, and the one the federal lane says is the only way into DSS-type money, is a **joint application led by an established Queensland DFV or women's service with ATHENA as the technology partner**.
4. **It has no trading revenue.** SEDI ($50,000–120,000, rolling to early 2027) needs more than $50,000 of trading revenue; the Lord Mayor's Women in Business Grant needs $75,000–500,000 turnover and a full financial year of trading; Westpac's and Snow's fellowships need an initiative that is live with evidence. These become 2027-28 targets once ATHENA is in market.

The next 90 days: **Startmate by 1 November**, incorporate and register for GST, apply for the in-kind credits, put the founder in front of Galileo, Blackbird and Rampersand, register for the QMHC sponsorship window (2–30 November) if a launch or wellbeing event is planned, join QSEC ($75) so the Queensland Treasury impact funds are heard about the day they open, and open the conversation with a DFV service partner before the Safe Futures window (11 January–8 February 2027).

## 3. Money that opens when the structure changes

### 3a. Incorporate — Pty Ltd, ABN, GST (do this first)

Everything in Section 1.1 and 1.2 and most of Section 2 assumes a company. The R&D Tax Incentive alone, on a launch year with, say, $150,000 of genuine AI-safety R&D spend, is roughly $65,000 back in cash after the tax return — the largest single non-dilutive source in this register, and it needs nothing but a company, records and eligible work. **ESIC status** (an Early Stage Innovation Company) gives every angel who buys new shares a 20% non-refundable offset and a capital-gains exemption, which is a real inducement for the first cheque; a new Queensland Pty Ltd with no income passes the early-stage test outright and the AI-safety platform is a credible principles-based innovation candidate. Register for GST voluntarily: CSIRO Kick-Start, Ignite Ideas and the Industry Growth Program require it.

### 3b. Women-led ownership and leadership

The condition is precise: at least 51% female ownership and a woman in a primary executive role, both held for six continuous months before applying, for the **Queensland Female Founders Co-Investment Fund** (A$50,000–200,000 of Queensland Government money co-invested alongside a private investor; open to 30 June 2027; post proof-of-concept; under A$500,000 raised; GST-registered). The same gate, in different words, is on **Scale Investors** (pre-seed A$100,000–500,000, up to 20%; Consumer Tech, Care Tech and AI-core for women's wellbeing are its stated focus — thematically one of the best matches in the register; needs a woman co-founder with equal equity and a C-suite role), **Coralus** (0% community loans; women/non-binary led; funding currently paused), the **Queensland Government's Backing Female Founders program** ($2.8m in the 2026-27 Budget's Women's Economic Security Statement; guidelines not yet published), **Startup Onramp's Female Founders places** and **Fishburners' Ascent Project** (both unconfirmed as running), the **AgriFutures Rural Women's Award** ($15,000; a woman applicant with a live business; closes 4 November 2026), **Women in Digital**, **AusMumpreneur**, **Cartier** and **Global Sisters** coaching. The **Lord Mayor's Women in Business Grant** ($5,000, closes 6 October 2026) is women-owned *and* needs $75,000–500,000 turnover with a full financial year of trading, so it is a 2027-28 round.

The honest reading of the register: a woman co-founder with equal equity and an executive title, brought on before launch, is worth more in reachable money than any single grant in this document, and the six-month clock means the value decays with every month of delay.

## 7. Method and limits, plainly

- Ten finders, 39 verification batches, one critic, four more verification batches: 54 agents. Every entry in Appendix A carries the URL actually read and a verification note saying what the finder got wrong.
- **The shared web-search budget was exhausted partway through.** Finders had 8–48 searches each before it ran out; the critic had none and worked only from official URLs it already knew. Discovery is therefore thinnest exactly where it should be thickest: the Queensland Government Grants Finder (a JavaScript app that returned nothing), GrantConnect's forecast list (walked in full by one agent before the budget ran out, blocked for the rest), the Community Grants Hub, SmartyGrants-hosted Queensland rounds and council sub-pages.
- **Bot protection.** advance.qld.gov.au, innovation.qld.gov.au, women.qld.gov.au, families.qld.gov.au, justice.qld.gov.au, dtet.qld.gov.au, ato.gov.au, grants.gov.au, communitygrants.gov.au, pmc.gov.au, esafety.gov.au and the Yunus Centre refused the fetcher. Statuses for those programs come from ministerial statements (statements.qld.gov.au), the 2026-27 Budget Women's Economic Security Statement, business.gov.au, SmartyGrants pages, QCOSS mirrors of guideline text, and named third-party listings; every such entry says so in its notes.
- The verifiers were instructed to be sceptical and default to "could not verify". Fourteen did. They also downgraded several finder claims (Ignite Ideas is not open; Innovate to Grow has no intake now; TEN13's minimum cheque; Sefa's $500,000 revenue floor; Scale's ownership test; Antler's no-solo-founders rule) and upgraded a few (Mentoring for Growth has a start-up session; QMHC sponsorship takes a Pty Ltd; NAB accepts for-profit social enterprises).
- Dates are as read on 30 September 2026 (AEST). Amounts for US programs are in US dollars as published.
- Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice; the R&D, ESIC and social-enterprise structuring points should be confirmed with an accountant before the company is set up, because the structure chosen determines which half of this register is reachable.
