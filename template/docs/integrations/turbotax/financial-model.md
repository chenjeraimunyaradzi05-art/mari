# TurboTax Integration — Financial Model (Summary)

This short financial model presents three commercial scenarios and high-level assumptions to guide partnership negotiations.

Assumptions (example pilot)
- Pilot users: 200 active trial users in year 1
- Conversion to paid tax product (pilot baseline): 5% → 10 paying users
- Average revenue per filing: $60 (referral share, co-branded fee, or ATHENA add-on split)
- Integration & development one-time cost: $200k (integration gateway, UX, compliance)
- Ongoing annual costs (support, infra, partner fees): $60k/yr

Scenario 1 — Referral / Affiliate (Lowest lift)
- Revenue: 200 users * 5% convert * $60 = $600
- Net revenue: revenue minus small referral cut and minimal costs (pilot cost amortized)
- Notes: Not revenue positive from pilot; fastest to market and low risk.

Scenario 2 — Subscription Add-on
- Pricing: $5/month add-on or $50/year
- Revenue: 200 users * 5% * $50 = $5000 annual
- If scaled: 5k MAU * 5% convert * $50 = $12,500/month ≈ $150k/yr
- Notes: Better long-term unit economics, requires UX & value-add.

Scenario 3 — Per-Filing (ATHENA handles filing or white-label)
- Pricing: $60 per filed return; revenue split 50/50 with TurboTax in partnership (example)
- Pilot: 200 users * 5% => 10 * $60 = $600 revenue (ATHENA share $300)
- Scaled: 5k MAU * 5% * $60 = $15k (ATHENA share $7.5k)
- Notes: Higher revenue per transaction; requires deeper legal & compliance readiness.

Sensitivity & break-even (simple)
- With $200k initial costs and $60k annual costs, to break even in 3 years we'd need aggressive scaling and higher conversion rates or higher revenue share.

Guidance
- Start with referral + projection pilot to validate conversion & user demand (low cost).
- Use pilot results to determine conversion % and LTV, then iterate on subscription or per-filing approaches for improved monetization.
- Include tracking in pilot to capture conversion to filing and any downstream product adoption.
